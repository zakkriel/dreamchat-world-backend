# 01 Product Vision and Promise

## Product One-Liner

A persistent AI RPG world where dynamic NPCs remember, relationships evolve, and time matters inside the story.

## Product Philosophy

A world that lives with you, not for you.

The app should not feel like a chatbot waiting to satisfy every user prompt. It should feel like a fictional world with its own weight, history, people, conflicts, secrets, and consequences.

The player is part of the world, but not automatically the center of it.

The player can become important through actions, relationships, power, reputation, choices, or selected story style. But by default, the world does not exist only to serve the player. It reacts, remembers, resists, changes, and continues through the fiction.

## Product Promise

The product promise is simple:

The user can enter a fictional RPG world, interact with dynamic NPCs, shape events through natural conversation, and return to a world that remembers what happened.

The app should create the feeling that:

- NPCs are not disposable.

- Relationships have history.

- Choices leave marks.

- Places can change.

- Time can move forward.

- The world has continuity.

The key emotional promise is:

This is not just a chat.  

This is your persistent RPG world.

## What the App Is

The app is a web-based AI RPG world experience.

Users can create or enter a fictional world, play through scenes with a narrator, interact with many NPCs, form relationships, trigger consequences, and let the story evolve over time.

The world contains characters, locations, groups, rumors, conflicts, events, memories, and unresolved threads.

NPCs can be created dynamically during play. Some may remain background characters. Others may become recurring characters, allies, rivals, enemies, lovers, companions, mentors, or core cast members depending on how the story develops.

The app should feel like an infinite RPG campaign with a living cast.

## What the App Is Not

The app is not mainly a companion app.

The app is not just an AI Dungeon clone.

The app is not only a D&D rules simulator.

The app is not a chatbot with genre decoration.

The app is not a writing assistant where the user passively generates prose.

The app is a persistent RPG world where the user plays inside the fiction and the world remembers what matters.

Companion-like experiences may happen naturally if users build deep relationships with NPCs. That is acceptable. But the core product positioning remains persistent AI RPG worlds, not AI companionship as the main pitch.

## Core Product Pillars

### 1. Persistent World

The world should maintain continuity across scenes and sessions.

Important events, relationships, promises, conflicts, locations, and consequences should remain part of the world after they happen.

### 2. Dynamic NPCs

NPCs can be created naturally during play.

A random guard, neighbor, merchant, politician, prisoner, executive, traveler, or witness can become meaningful if the story gives them weight.

NPCs should not all start with the same depth. Their importance should grow based on relevance, interaction, and narrative consequence.

### 3. Memory That Matters

The app should remember the things that make the world feel real.

Not every small detail needs the same importance. The product should prioritize memories that affect identity, relationships, promises, conflicts, secrets, reputation, commitments, and emotional history.

### 4. Time Inside the Story

Time does not pass because the user was offline.

Time passes when it passes inside the fiction.

For example:

- the user-controlled character travels for three weeks

- the story jumps to winter

- a crisis lasts six months

- the character returns to a place after two in-world years

- an entity has not been seen for a long period of story time

When in-world time passes, the world may change.

### 5. Backstage Updates

Backstage Updates are the product mechanism for showing that the world changed during in-world time gaps.

If an entity, group, location, or conflict has been away from the current scene for a long period of story time, the app should update what happened before the next meaningful interaction.

The depth of the update should depend on relevance.

A background entity may only need a small status update.  

A former close ally may need a full life update, emotional shift, relationship consequence, and new unresolved thread.

### 6. Narrator-Led RPG Experience

The narrator is the user’s window into the world.

The narrator describes scenes, frames consequences, manages tone, gives weight to time, and helps orchestrate multiple NPCs.

The narrator should not be a passive assistant. It should represent the world.

But the user should still have control through correction, canon override, difficulty settings, and story preferences.

### 7. User-Controlled Canon

The user should be able to correct the world when something is wrong.

If an NPC forgets something important, if a past event is misrepresented, or if the narrator makes a bad assumption, the user should have a clear way to correct it.

Correction should feel like world direction, not debugging.

## Default Player Position

The default experience is not “you are the chosen one.”

The default experience is:

You are a person inside a world that has its own weight.

You may become powerful.  

You may become central.  

You may become feared, loved, worshipped, hunted, ignored, or forgotten.  

But this should emerge from the story or be chosen intentionally by the user.

This creates a stronger world fantasy.

The world should not always orbit the player by default. But the product may later support different story styles where the player is more central, more powerful, or more protected by the narrative.

A parked product concept here is a `world_relevance_score`: a way to express how much the world currently considers or orbits the user-controlled character/entity. A low relevance world evolves more independently. A high relevance world pulls more events, NPCs, and consequences toward the user-controlled character/entity.

## Mature Creative Freedom

The app should support mature fictional worlds.

That includes death, violence, horror, gore, strong language, sex, morally dark stories, intense relationships, betrayal, trauma, political conflict, and adult themes where appropriate to user settings and legal/safety boundaries.

The product should not be sanitized into a generic fantasy toy.

But mature content is not the core pitch by itself.

The core pitch remains:

Persistent RPG worlds.  

Dynamic NPCs.  

Memory.  

Consequences.  

Meaningful time.

## PoC Alignment

The first proof of concept should validate the smallest version of the product promise.

The PoC should include:

- one world

- one user-controlled character/entity

- one narrator/facilitator

- 5–10 meaningful NPCs/entities that can be managed

- a few locations

- a visible timeline

- a relationship system

- memory/canon correction UX

- strong resume-later experience

- backstage updates based on in-world time progression

The PoC succeeds if one small world feels alive, remembered, and changed across time.

Not huge.  

Not infinite.  

Not a marketplace.  

Not multiplayer.  

Not a complete rules engine.

Just one world that feels persistent enough to believe in.

## Success Definition

This product succeeds when users feel:

I remember this world.  

This world remembers me.  

The NPCs have history with me.  

My choices changed things.  

Time mattered.  

The world feels bigger than me, but I can still affect it.
